# Interview depth and producer follow-through

The interview should turn broad judgments into useful examples, practices and lessons without depending on a participant volunteering every detail. There is no minimum interview duration. Participant boundaries and requests to stop take priority.

## Cue delivery update

Judge the interview by whether a grounded follow-up elicits a concrete lesson the participant had not yet articulated: an action, consequence, tradeoff, or practice another team can use. This is guidance for Sam and Sol, not a new score or a requirement to find a lesson in every answer.

Follow-ups should be surgical: connect a detail already supplied to a consequential point still unexplained. A practice plus its effect should not be repackaged as generic advice, but it can still contain an unresolved decision, tradeoff, or contradiction worth probing. Routine orientation and broad topic changes belong to Sam; Sol adds a specific missed question or interviewing correction. A pending direction must not become an extra question tacked onto a newer story.

Remove the final Jev cue check. Sol selects direction; Sam receives it and decides how to weave it into the current conversation, deferring during a useful story and skipping answered or inappropriate questions. Retain session lifetime, generation deadlines, budgets, spacing, and cancellation of superseded recovery work. Research identity checks remain separate.

Apply the same division of responsibility to Simulator actor cues: no post-generation applicability or resolution veto. Keep the changed-dialogue check for hints shown directly to the trainee. Preserve after-delivery observation, which distinguishes useful deferral from an ignored direction.

Verify delivery despite new speech, preserved cancellation/budgets, unchanged research checks and trainee hints, and grounded producer questions in paid synthetic replays. Refresh affected Debugger examples from measured results.

## Implementation

1. Replace broad anti-drilling guidance with grounded follow-ups for a missing example, action, reason or consequence. Preserve concise complete answers and avoid leading questions.
2. Make Sol directions firm and single-purpose. Deliver them through `session.instructions.append`; research and coverage remain factual context through `session.thinking.append`.
3. Assess the latest accepted cue within the existing Jev interviewer call: followed, deferred, missed, retired or not yet assessable. Keep provider acknowledgment separate from observed behavior. Preserve timing and evidence in the private archive.
4. Allow one Sol reconsideration for a confidently missed cue, after ordinary cue spacing. Reuse the existing bounded producer orchestration. A replacement inherits the limit. Stale or superseded work cannot reassert an old cue.
5. Hide the completed-topic list from Sam's private rundown while retaining participant coverage UI and Sol's full evidence. Correct narrow wrong-party grading and analogy-based research errors.

## Verification

Run targeted timing, supersession, recovery, boundary, archive and summary tests; refresh anonymized Jev recordings; run real Sol replays; rehearse a quiet participant and controlled directions through real voice. Judge actual subsequent questions and listen to the audio. Provider receipt is not proof of use. Finish with the full check and WebRTC/streamed-summary/private-archive acceptance, then publish only to the existing isolated interview preview.

## Review refinements

Instruction delivery now repeats the essential priority rules: first finish a fresh useful story, apply the latest relevant direction once, and respect an answered question, a boundary or a request to stop. It must not trigger speech merely on receipt. The same wrapper is used in the voice rehearsal.

Timing checks require a substantive participant-to-Sam exchange, exclude one-frame trailing words, and allow the small timestamp overlap observed at ordinary turn boundaries. An exhausted recovery stops being reassessed, while an independent boundary correction survives a stale recovery in the same consultation. Session tests cover both accepted and rejected instruction events and private archive wiring.

Synthetic recordings were refreshed for rubric v7. Real voice rehearsals cover quiet answers, new disclosures, already-answered questions, cues arriving during speech, supersession, and explicit stops. Provider receipt alone is never counted as behavioral success. The former cue-delivery check withheld useful directions in some replays; the update above removes that veto rather than adjusting its threshold. Coverage remains probabilistic. The follow-through judgment is diagnostic and does not score the participant.
