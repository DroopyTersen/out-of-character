# Proposed character grounding

September 18, 2026. Adopted for live Jev judgments at Andrew’s request after the controlled comparison. Player-facing character text is unchanged. These two-sentence additions explain the priorities and behavior underneath each joke for the judge. They supplement the exact curated name and backstory, rather than replace either.

The same prose is available as [typed character grounding](../../core/character-grounding.ts), shared by the Storybook character workshop and every live Jev question. Both full-transcript and recent-window evaluations include it.

The judge should still evaluate what the speaker personally expresses or defends. A short recognizable decision can be sufficient; players do not need to repeat the backstory, say particular product names, supply numbers, or reproduce every trait. Nearby personas can both fit when the speaker actually enacts both. The distinctions below narrow generic workplace attitudes without making the cast mutually exclusive.

## Grounding for the current 44-character cast

Names, IDs, and order follow [the current cast](../../core/characters.ts), checked against [the curated character document](../consultancy-party-game-characters.md).

### The Shiny Stack Evangelist — `shiny-stack-evangelist`

They want to be associated with whatever technology has just become fashionable and feel embarrassed by a working system that looks dated. They push replacement because the new stack is exciting and prestigious, rather than because they have found a concrete defect in the old code.

### The Graybeard — `graybeard`

They prize their hard-earned experience and resent people presenting familiar ideas as discoveries. They challenge a supposedly new design by explaining how they tried it before and why it failed, rather than simply refusing to leave an existing system.

### The Brownfield Lifer — `brownfield-lifer`

They want to keep working the existing system's tickets and reject invitations to a transformative rewrite as another distracting crusade. They stubbornly refuse the journey itself; ordinary advice to make one change simple or ship something useful does not establish this persona.

### The Refactor Missionary — `refactor-missionary`

They treat untidy existing code as an unacceptable wrong that must be corrected before even a small requested change can proceed. They expand the work into cleanup or restructuring and defend the delay as necessary purification, rather than chasing a fashionable replacement for its own sake.

### The Architecture Astronaut — `architecture-astronaut`

They care more about an elaborate system design than the immediate thing the user needs to do. They postpone or delegate the working feature while insisting on architectural machinery and approval of their design, rather than merely polishing an impressive slide.

### The Millisecond Martyr — `millisecond-martyr`

They want recognition for making something faster even when nobody has a meaningful problem with its speed. They demand that useful feature work yield to their optimization and treat the improvement as an achievement the room owes them admiration for.

### The CSS Stunt Pilot — `css-stunt-pilot`

They take pride in pulling off a visually clever layout that other people cannot safely understand or alter. They defend the trick or their exclusive ability to maintain it even when a small adjustment makes the layout break, rather than simply caring about attractive design.

### The Platform Hall Monitor — `platform-hall-monitor`

They believe compliance with the platform team's approved implementation patterns gives a change permission to exist. They block an obvious solution until its author follows the sanctioned route, rather than requiring sprint ceremonies or arguing that the work costs extra.

### The Azure Icon Collagist — `azure-icon-collagist`

They want a cloud presentation to look sophisticated and impressive, with little concern for whether they can explain what it accomplishes. They choose services and embellish the slide for its appearance, rather than insisting that an elaborate architecture must be built before a feature can ship.

### The Certification Peacock — `certification-peacock`

They want credentials to establish their importance and authority without having to demonstrate practical experience. They bring the conversation back to badges or qualifications when asked what they have actually delivered, rather than earning status through a working solution.

### The SharePoint Civilian — `sharepoint-civilian`

They trust familiar configurable business tools to get the department's work done and distrust the burden of a custom application. They reject bespoke development in favor of a workable list or view, rather than protecting secret formulas or retaliating against IT for taking over their personal creation.

### The Excel Warlord — `excel-warlord`

They want their workbook to remain the business's real authority and their own knowledge to remain indispensable. They withhold its logic or refuse a replacement because everyone must keep coming to them for the truth, rather than merely preferring a familiar tool.

### The Low-Code Freedom Fighter — `low-code-freedom-fighter`

They are committed to preserving the promise that the solution frees the client from developers, even when it plainly depends on developer rescue. They excuse fragile automation and keep selling the success story instead of admitting that the promised independence has failed.

### The 30,000-Footer — `30000-footer`

They want to remain in the comfortable world of strategy and alignment where nobody can pin them to a concrete answer. They turn a practical question into another abstract discussion or workshop and dismiss specifics as beneath the conversation's altitude.

### The Backlog Stenographer — `backlog-stenographer`

They consider faithfully recording what the client said more important than resolving whether the requests make sense together. They turn contradictory demands into deliverable work and defend the record as correct, rather than seeking a decision about which demand should win.

### The Rabbit-Hole QA — `rabbit-hole-qa`

They want an unusual failure path to receive attention far beyond its practical importance. They expose it to the client or insist it dominate the work even when normal use suffers, rather than treating a reassuring testing measure as proof that a real bug does not matter.

### The Scrum Cop — `scrum-cop`

They treat following the team's ceremonies as a prerequisite for legitimate work even during an urgent incident. They interrupt a needed repair to enforce refinement, planning, or the board, rather than using Agile ceremonies to disguise an inflexible commercial commitment.

### The Checked-Out PM — `checked-out-pm`

They want to present reassuring project status while avoiding the effort of understanding or managing what is happening. They assert that things are fine and ask others to supply the substance of their report, rather than merely being an absent sponsor or silent attendee.

### The Estimate Barber — `estimate-barber`

They want a client-pleasing delivery promise even when engineering has explained that the work takes longer. They shorten the commitment without reducing the work and shift responsibility back to engineering when reality catches up, rather than conceding on the price of the people doing it.

### The Pre-Sales Illusionist — `pre-sales-illusionist`

They want the buyer to believe the impressive demonstration is already close to a real working solution. They hide staged behavior or dismiss unresolved integration and security as minor finishing work, rather than being the buyer who refuses to fund those necessities.

### The Staffing Magician — `staffing-magician`

They want a staffing gap to appear solved more than they want the chosen person to be capable of the work. They present thin familiarity and limited availability as adequate coverage, rather than simply being eager to get themselves assigned or pushing everyone to bill more.

### The Code Coverage TSA — `code-coverage-tsa`

They want a favorable testing measure to count as quality regardless of whether the system behaves correctly. They defend release readiness with the report while dismissing an observable bug; saying that testing is important or describing a report alone is insufficient.

### The UX Safari Guide — `ux-safari-guide`

They want even a small interface decision to justify a substantial research expedition. They defer a straightforward improvement for more discovery while a concrete usability problem persists, rather than simply listening to users before a consequential design choice.

### The Accessibility Scold — `accessibility-scold`

They want an accessibility defect treated as evidence of the team's moral failure, not just a problem to fix. They turn the correction into an extended rebuke or demand for repentance; calmly identifying an accessibility barrier does not establish this persona.

### The Reorg Bard — `reorg-bard`

They want their survival through organizational upheaval and memory of how things used to work to make them the keeper of the story. They answer a practical question with sprawling institutional history while leaving the needed knowledge unwritten, rather than proving that a new technical idea is old.

### The Utilization Hawk — `utilization-hawk`

They view people primarily as billable capacity to fill and keep filled. They respond to either spare time or an already full workload by pushing more account work, rather than considering the person's development or whether a particular assignment is a good fit.

### The JPEG Sponsor — `jpeg-sponsor`

They occupy the visible position of executive sponsor without providing the attention or decisions the project needs. They send an uninformed substitute or evade participation while retaining the sponsoring role, rather than actively maintaining a misleading project status report.

### The Manager Still in the PR — `manager-still-in-the-pr`

They want to retain personal control of the code after becoming responsible for growing and trusting other people. They hold up delivery by refusing to let the team change their repository without their involvement, rather than enforcing an impersonal platform standard.

### The Fake Mentor — `fake-mentor`

They want a supposedly supportive conversation to meet their own need to vent rather than the other person's need to develop. They redirect the discussion to their grievances and reduce the person's growth plan to staying billable, rather than openly managing utilization alone.

### The Desperately Available — `desperately-available`

They want escape from the bench so badly that any assignment with a charge code sounds wonderful. They volunteer themselves for unsuitable work or implausible availability and insist they are thrilled, rather than assigning someone else to paper over a staffing gap.

### The Rate Card Doormat — `rate-card-doormat`

They want to avoid disappointing the client in a price negotiation even at the expense of the value of the people being sold. They concede an unreasonable rate for senior expertise before defending it, rather than cutting the time needed to deliver the work.

### The Out-of-Scope PM — `out-of-scope-pm`

They want the contract's omissions to give them a reason to refuse even an obvious part of a useful solution. They turn the request into a scope or commercial argument despite vague original commitments, rather than blocking it over engineering patterns or sprint process.

### The Hit-and-Run Architect — `hit-and-run-architect`

They want to declare the solution straightforward and move on before learning enough to own the consequences. They prescribe a confident architecture after shallow discovery and leave the delivery team to correct expectations, rather than staying to demand more architectural machinery.

### The Entra Jenga Player — `entra-jenga-player`

They want their precarious identity configuration to remain untouched and their fixes to remain defensible. They add awkward exceptions and rationalize resulting sign-in failures as normal, rather than deliberately restricting device capabilities as a desired outcome.

### The Laptop Jailer — `laptop-jailer`

They consider preventing users from doing things a successful device-management outcome even when ordinary work becomes miserable. They defend broad restrictions as the intended design, rather than excusing accidental lockouts caused by fragile identity rules.

### The Teams Evangelist — `teams-evangelist`

They want their preferred collaboration system and naming rules to govern every conversation. They demand more channels and blame people's difficulty finding things on failure to follow their convention, rather than reconsidering whether the system is helping.

### The Lakehouse Tour Guide — `lakehouse-tour-guide`

They want the data platform's impressive concepts to count as progress even while the business gets its usable answers elsewhere. They keep touring the platform instead of addressing why finance still relies on its familiar export, rather than personally guarding that spreadsheet's formulas.

### The Ignite Tourist (Client) — `ignite-tourist`

They want a keynote's effortless promise to become their own project immediately without reconciling it with delivery constraints. They demand the showcased capability on the existing deadline and dismiss the difficulty because it looked easy on stage, rather than advocating a new framework for personal technical prestige.

### The Shadow-IT Founder (Client) — `shadow-it-founder`

They want to retain autonomy and ownership over a solution they built outside official IT. They resist or undermine the sanctioned replacement as an unwelcome takeover, rather than simply arguing that a familiar business tool is sufficient.

### The Security No-Show (Client) — `security-no-show`

They want to exercise approval authority late while avoiding responsibility for missing the opportunities to participate earlier. They introduce a preventable security blocker near completion and deny being included, rather than enforcing a rule through an ongoing review process.

### The Magic-Only Buyer (Client) — `magic-only-buyer`

They want the impressive visible outcome without paying for the necessary work that makes it real and reliable. They reject funding for migration, logging, or other enabling work as somebody else's IT problem; merely favoring a useful feature over unnecessary complexity is insufficient.

### The Mute-Button Salesman — `mute-button-salesman`

They want the recognition of being involved without contributing anything substantive to the conversation. They offer an empty late apology or response and still expect stakeholder credit, rather than claiming to manage the project or providing sponsorship decisions.

### The Reply-All Disaster Artist — `reply-all-disaster-artist`

They feel entitled to air a pointed response to an audience that does not need to be part of the dispute. They escalate the tension by deliberately involving the wider thread, rather than simply using reply-all for information relevant to everyone.

### The Fake-Agile Dad — `fake-agile-dad`

They want the Agile label while retaining complete certainty and control over scope, date, and budget. They point to ceremonies as proof of flexibility but forbid adaptation and demand a rigid schedule, rather than merely insisting that the team follow its process.

## Eight close confusion pairs and candidate probes

These are proposed new comparison inputs, not provider-tested fixtures. Each pair holds the topic roughly constant and changes the speaker's priority; expected behavior is defined before any run. Neither quoted input needs a numerical claim or the character's name. The same transcript must be sent to the current questions and the proposed questions so that a change in performance text cannot masquerade as a judging improvement.

| Pair | Transcript A and expected enactment | Transcript B and expected enactment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Brownfield Lifer / Graybeard | **Brownfield:** “Leave me out of your transformation programme. I'm staying with the old system's tickets, whatever you rename the rewrite.” | **Graybeard:** “Your revolutionary design is the same one we tried years ago. Sit down; I'll explain why it failed then.” |
| Architecture Astronaut / Azure Icon Collagist | **Astronaut:** “The customer screen can wait. We need the service boundaries and orchestration approved before anybody builds that feature.” | **Collagist:** “Put a few more cloud services on the slide so it looks substantial. What the application actually does isn't my part of the presentation.” |
| Shiny Stack Evangelist / Refactor Missionary | **Evangelist:** “It works, but I can't have my name on something using that tired stack. We're replacing it with the framework everyone is talking about.” | **Missionary:** “I'm not changing that label while this component is such a mess. Clean up its structure first; the user can wait.” |
| Scrum Cop / Fake-Agile Dad | **Scrum Cop:** “The outage repair hasn't been through refinement. Nobody touches it until we have followed the proper ceremony.” | **Fake-Agile Dad:** “We have stand-ups, so we're Agile. The date, scope and budget are fixed, and I won't accept any changes.” |
| Estimate Barber / Rate Card Doormat | **Estimate Barber:** “I know the team needs longer, but I've already promised the earlier date. They'll have to make it happen and explain themselves if they don't.” | **Rate Card Doormat:** “You want our most experienced person at the junior rate? Absolutely, that's fine; I wouldn't want this to become an awkward negotiation.” |
| Staffing Magician / Desperately Available | **Staffing Magician:** “They watched someone use the tool and have an afternoon free. I've marked the specialist role as covered.” | **Desperately Available:** “I've been waiting for an account for ages. I'll learn whatever it is tonight; please just give me something I can bill.” |
| SharePoint Civilian / Shadow-IT Founder | **Civilian:** “A list and a sensible view will do this job. I don't want us stuck maintaining another custom application.” | **Founder:** “I built the team's app without IT and it's mine. I'm telling people to ignore their official replacement; they don't get to take this over.” |
| Pre-Sales Illusionist / Magic-Only Buyer | **Illusionist:** “The demo looked seamless because I staged the data. Tell the buyer the real integration is just a finishing touch.” | **Buyer:** “I'll fund the impressive feature, but not the migration or the logging it needs. Those are your IT chores; find another budget.” |

For each pair, add one plain same-topic negative that expresses neither distinctive priority. Suitable negatives include discussing an old design without dismissing novelty, choosing a service for a real requirement, fixing a label without expanding the job, following ceremonies without obstructing urgent work, negotiating an honest estimate and rate, assigning a suitably qualified person, choosing a business tool cooperatively, and agreeing to fund the feature's necessary integration. Product mentions and generic good practice should remain insufficient.

Add a separate shared boundary input: “Let's build the useful feature first and keep the design simple. We still need to pay for the migration and support work that make it usable.” It should enact neither the Magic-Only Buyer nor the Brownfield Lifer. This distinguishes pragmatic prioritization from withholding necessary funding and refusing transformation. Also retain the existing generic-pragmatism regression verbatim; its previously observed confusion makes it a regression case, not a new holdout.

## Minimal comparison procedure

1. Review and settle the grounding before running it. Freeze the current question version, the proposed question version, the cast, provider version, and comparison inputs; do not change thresholds to make a variant look better.
2. Obtain independent short paraphrases of the eight pairs and their plain negatives after the wording is settled. Freeze their expected persona IDs before viewing outputs and reserve them as the held-out set. The examples in this document have helped design the grounding, so report them as development probes rather than claiming they are unseen validation.
3. Run the exact same frozen inputs against both question variants in Noul, keeping the existing fixture and Brownfield regression texts unchanged. Inspect all 44 readings: a high target reading alone does not reveal whether a neighboring persona also became a false match. Allow multiple positive labels only when their enacted priorities were specified in advance.
4. Review whether the intended character remains recognizable, whether its neighbor is being inferred from generic shared language, and whether the plain negatives stay uncharacteristic. Include short performances and varied everyday phrasing so the additions do not accidentally demand the entire joke or its signature words.
5. Report the comparison's improvements, regressions, unresolved confusions, and added request size. Retain the measured comparison and its limitations when adopting additions; documentation coverage is not behavioral validation.

The [existing Jev experiment](jev-spike.md) provides limited saved evidence and an observed generic-pragmatism boundary. This proposal makes no claim about untested cast accuracy, live microphone behavior, or changes in latency and cost. It adds no UI rules, numerical requirements, or player-facing exposition.
